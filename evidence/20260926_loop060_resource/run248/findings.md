# Run248 graph traffic attribution and sensitivity

The latest Run247 cohort has 16 gated rank-cycle target windows. Re-reading each exported CSV and trace, this analysis reconciles every window's AIC+AIV read bytes against Run247 within 10 kB. Summing family medians gives 18.964 GB read and 2.380 GB written per rank-cycle; this differs slightly from the median of per-window totals because medians do not distribute over sums.

The Run247 3.618 GB read `other` group consists mainly of 1.149 GB plain matmul, 0.639 GB transpose batch matmul, 0.618 GB inplace copy, 0.756 GB kernels with AivKernel/HcPre/HcPost names, 0.097 GB indexer and 0.357 GB remaining. The AivKernel/Hc* grouping is a naming inference; 265 AivKernel and 265 HCCL tasks per target window suggest association but do not establish one-to-one causality or link traffic. Profiler `communication.json` reports zero transit size for the inspected graph HCCL tasks, so link bytes remain unavailable.

Dividing 21.344 GB of gross reported read+write counters by hypothetical 1.0/1.3/1.6 TB/s gives 21.344/16.419/13.340 ms. This is only a dimensional sensitivity. Reported task counters may include repeat reads and are not unique compulsory bytes; available device bandwidth on this mixed, dependent graph is unknown; profiled target scopes and kernel-duration sums include synchronization and overlap. None of the three times is a proved lower bound or an exposed E2E saving. In particular, subtracting 16.419 ms from the 54.796 ms profiled target median would be invalid.

The GMM real graph read/active-packed ratio of 1.092 leaves little gross amplification to attack in its weight path. The largest measurable non-GMM traffic families are quant matmul (3.465 GB), DSA/cache compression (1.530 GB), sparse attention (1.062 GB), plain/transpose matmul (1.788 GB combined) and inplace copy (0.618 GB). The next useful experiment is a same-shape, same-dependency profiling/intervention on one of these families, with official fixed-serving correctness and repeated formal E2E as the final judge. At present the hardware-attainable product throughput bound remains unidentified.
