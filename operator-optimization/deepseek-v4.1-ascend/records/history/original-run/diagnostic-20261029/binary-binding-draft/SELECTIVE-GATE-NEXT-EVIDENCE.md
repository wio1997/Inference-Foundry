# Selective recorder with a PROFILE_ACTIVE window

This is a separate candidate implementation, `selective_recorder_next_gate.c`, for later review. The active diagnostic container and logs were not altered.

At startup, observed binary digests and function/handle associations are retained in fixed-size process-local tables. Matching load/function events and target launch/graph events are suppressed while `WIO_DIAG_DIR/PROFILE_ACTIVE` is absent and the directory is observable. When the marker exists as a regular file, an observed matching launch can emit its cached binary digest and handle generation. A missing/unreadable directory, invalid marker, or absent environment produces `gate_unknown`, a sticky `state_incomplete`, and no launch records. Gate observation is at wrapper entry; it is neither a precise profiler boundary nor synchronization with the NPU. The marker can change between the file check and the real call.

Early ByEntry launches are retained for later candidate reclassification only when their launch wrapper observes an active marker. Ring/batch losses and reclassifications suppressed after a window closes taint completeness. The cached timestamp describes the launch wrapper's observation. A later matching active window is not necessarily the same window; the current recorder does not establish window identity. Logs can reach their cap even during the active period, and missed statistics, termination, or other loss must be treated as unknown.

The implementation marks `launch_mutation_overlaps` and sticky `state_incomplete` if observed wrapper intervals for a launch and binary load/get/unload overlap. This detects a class of lifecycle races, not all of them: the interposer may miss internal or alternate entry points, asynchronous runtime behavior, and reuse beyond observed wrappers. A launch selected by a cached handle before the real call can therefore still refer to a different binary or function. Every association remains `candidate_unverified`; a pre-call digest cannot establish the runtime's actual loaded bytes.

Nested graph capture observations are candidates, not proof of graph/task/kernel bindings. In particular, if graph capture happens only during warmup and the measured window only replays it, this draft may not associate the replay with a target launch at all. Graph APIs may also be invisible to this interposer. This limits the value of another capture: positive name/handle/launch records could narrow the runtime path; absence of those records cannot establish that DispatchFFNCombine did not run, nor identify its profiler task. A decision to recapture should account for this limitation and the overhead of gate file checks and hashing.

CPU-only mock result in the user's stage container: `next-selective-revision5/next-selective-tests/gate.UlPobq` passed `-Wall -Wextra -Werror` build, 12,000 target warmup launches plus 3 active launches with a 4,186-byte log and cached pre-call hash, missing-directory unknown handling, and one launch/unload overlap. This does not establish production ABI coverage, NPU behavior, inference semantics or performance overhead. Initial mock compile failed on a formatting warning and passed after fixing the test; no NPU was started.
